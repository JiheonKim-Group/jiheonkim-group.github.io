A decarbonization technology is, at its core, a high-dimensional optimization problem. From material synthesis parameters and the resulting active materials, through the microenvironment around them, to system parameters and device operating conditions, dozens of coupled variables shape the final performance in practice. Exploring this space one factor at a time is slow, and the resulting discovery gap is a major reason why promising materials rarely reach deployment.

Our goal is to navigate this space far faster with the self-driving laboratory described above: robotic experimentation to generate data at scale, AI to decide the next experiment, and human domain expertise to shape the platform and refine those decisions. Along the way, the accumulated experimental data let us build a bridge between computation and experiment, which have largely developed in parallel.
